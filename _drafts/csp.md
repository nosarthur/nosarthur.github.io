Crystal polymorphism, the ability of a compound to crystallize in multiple crystal forms, is a prevalent phenomenon. These polymorphs (crystal forms) exhibit diverse physical and chemical properties, including density, melting point, hardness, color, stability, morphology, solubility, and bioavailability. Therefore, understanding polymorphism is crucial to mitigate risks associated with unexpected polymorphs in industries like the pharmaceuticals, agrisciences, nutraceuticals, battery, and aviation. In pharmaceuticals, for instance, late-appearing polymorphs or those arising from altered production conditions can significantly impact product quality, efficacy, and safety, leading to patent disputes, regulatory issues, and even market recalls. To address this challenge, we developed a novel crystal structure prediction (CSP) method for molecular crystals, offering both exceptional accuracy and efficiency. Our method features a systematic divide-and-conquer crystal packing search algorithm, built upon space group symmetry decompositions, as well as machine learning force fields for hierarchical energy ranking. This high accuracy and reliability positions our method for routine crystal structure prediction in drug formulation, accelerating clinical formulation design and de-risking downstream manufacturing processes.
